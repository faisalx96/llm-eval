# qym Platform — Design Critique

> **Method.** This critique judged the **running product**, not the source. The platform was deployed locally (Postgres + FastAPI) and seeded with realistic data through the real SDK ingest path — two populated projects, 11 runs across 4 models in every lifecycle state (running, completed, failed, submitted, approved, rejected), item-level corrections, a versioned golden dataset, and one empty project. Every screen was walked at 1440px and 390px (~55 captures in [tmp/design-audit/screens](tmp/design-audit/screens)). A 22-agent review panel then critiqued the captures across seven dimensions, **adversarially re-verified 56 major claims against the live app** (several were refuted or corrected — those are excluded or amended below), and scored independently. Everything below survived verification.
>
> *Date: 2026-06-11*

---

## 1. Ground truth: what this product is for

qym is an LLM-evaluation platform: a Python SDK runs evals (task × dataset × metrics, usually per-model) and streams results into this self-hosted web app — the team's shared surface for results, diagnosis, and governance.

| User | Frequency | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **AI engineer** (primary) | daily | "Did my change make quality better or worse?" → open run, scan failures, drill into items/traces, compare vs baseline |
| **Team lead / reviewer** | weekly | "Is this run good enough to sign off?" → review submitted runs, approve/reject; review item-level corrections |
| **Eval maintainer** | ongoing | curate golden datasets, versions/aliases; watch model regressions over time |
| **Platform admin** | rare | users, projects, trash |

The dominant loop — the thing every structural choice must serve — is: **SDK run → open run detail → scan failures → compare vs baseline → decide.**

---

## 2. Overall verdict

Bar: *would a designer with authority at Linear / Stripe / Vercel / Notion have shipped these structural choices?* Scores are the consensus of three independent judges calibrated so 9–10 = ships at those companies today.

| Axis | Score | One-line verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **① Effectiveness / fitness** | **4 / 10** | The daily engineer's first half-loop is genuinely well-built; the other three jobs are structurally failed — no reviewer queue, no time axis anywhere, failures buried below aggregates |
| **② Coherence / consistency** | **4.5 / 10** | A real design *language* (dark, mono, teal), but not a design *system* — status colors, destructive actions, tabs, and filters are re-decided per page |
| **③ Craft / polish** | **5 / 10** | Distinctive identity with real moments of taste, undermined by color anarchy, unproofed copy, shipped mojibake, and zero responsive design |

**What kind of product this is:** *right domain instincts, no editorial system* — an engineer-built workbench with several deeply correct patterns, assembled by template accretion (stat-card hero + sections, stamped onto every page including the trash can) rather than shaped around the four jobs. It was clearly built by someone who understands eval work and has visual taste, and equally clearly never walked through by someone with the authority to say "this page answers no question."

---

## 3. Tier ① — Is it the RIGHT design?

### Philosophy

There *is* a philosophy visible: dense, terminal-dark, data-forward, SDK-first (the empty state shows a CLI command, not a fake "Create run" button — exactly right). But the philosophy governs the *skin*, not the *structure*. Structure was generated by two mechanical rules — "every page = KPI cards + content blocks" and "every chart type = a nav item" — which is how you get KPI cards on the trash page ("VISIBLE ROWS: 0", "ACCESS LEVEL: ADMIN" — a stat card telling me my own role, [05-trash.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/05-trash.png)) and three sibling analysis pages that are fragments of one question.

### Navigation & IA

The bones are right and deserve credit: project-scoped workspaces, and **project root lands directly on the Runs table** — the actual workbench — two clicks from cold. The dataset page is the best-modeled object in the product (Items / Runs / Lineage / Settings tabs, version pill with a Production alias, [21-dataset-detail.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/21-dataset-detail.png)).

The misses:

- **The reviewer's queue does not exist, and the nav lies about it.** The sidebar item named "Reviews" is the *item-corrections* queue ([17-reviews.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/17-reviews.png)). Run approval — the headline governance feature — lives as an unlabeled document-icon dropdown at the far right of a table row, **past the horizontal scroll at 1440px** (verified live: the SUBMITTED row carries a hidden "Review" popover with Approve/Reject). There is no "runs awaiting my sign-off" surface anywhere; the Dashboard shows *Approved* runs — governance output — but not the queue. A team lead clicking "Reviews" lands in the wrong workflow.
  **Better:** one "Review" section, two tabs — *Run approvals* (queue of SUBMITTED runs, approve/reject with required-comment dialog) and *Corrections* — plus Approve/Reject as first-class header actions on the run detail of any SUBMITTED run.
- **"Dashboard" is a worse copy of Runs.** Its tables are the Runs list minus columns and filters; its only unique slot is an empty box literally labeled "7-day trend chart" ([16-overview.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/16-overview.png)). Its topline numbers also contradict the Runs page one click away (5 runs / 81.7% vs 6 runs / 95.8%, definitions unlabeled).
  **Better:** kill it; pin live runs atop Runs, badge pending approvals on the Review nav item.
- **Charts / Models / Dashboard are three nav items decomposed by chart type, not by question.** All three partially answer "how are my models doing," each with different filter semantics.
  **Better:** one Analysis surface — *Trends* + *Leaderboard* — sharing one scope control.
- **Context-stranding routes.** `/compare?runs=…` and legacy `/run/{id}` render with the global sidebar — no project nav, no breadcrumb, UUID as the title (verified live in fresh sessions). In a team tool where pasting run links is the core collaboration act, a shared link is a navigational dead end.
  **Better:** canonical project-scoped URLs with redirects.

### Pattern-fit, surface by surface

- **Runs list: right skeleton, column budget spent backwards.** A dense table is correct here. But the widest column is a wrapping 60-char auto-slug (`support-rag-claude-sonnet-4-6-claude-sonnet-4-6-260611-1043`) that triple-duplicates the TASK, MODEL, and DATE columns — while DATE (the sort key!), OWNER, DURATION, and all row actions sit **off-screen right** at 1440px ([10-runs-list.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/10-runs-list.png) vs [79-runs-scrolled-right.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/79-runs-scrolled-right.png)). Two zero-entropy columns (VERSION, DATASET — identical in every row) burn the width that recency needed.
  **Better:** human-label run names with middle-ellipsis, auto-hide constant columns, pin a kebab actions column right.
- **Run detail: an inverted pyramid.** The page order is verdict → latency/Trace Stats → Performance by Category → Error Distribution → Root Cause → *and only then* the items. On a passing run, you scroll past a ~200px box that says "No errors" and another that says "No root causes" to reach the actual work object ([70-run-approved-scroll-1.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/70-run-approved-scroll-1.png)). And the items are a **feed of ~430px full cards — two items per viewport.** At 12 items it's already a 5,000px scroll; at a realistic 300-item golden set the pattern is dead. The user guide even calls it an "items table"; the shipped UI isn't one.
  **Better:** verdict band above the fold, then a dense failures-first table with row-expand (or master–detail), and the analysis sections collapsed into a rail.
- **Compare: the best pattern in the app, buried under the worst opening.** The item-by-item layout — shared INPUT/EXPECTED rendered once, per-run output columns side-by-side with verdicts, latency, and inline root-cause cards ([75-compare-scroll-4.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/75-compare-scroll-4.png)) — is exactly the right structure for "where do they differ," and most eval tools never get it. But the page *opens* with three cards repeating eight identical metadata fields, then a winner-matrix where ties at 100% and rows of zero-telemetry (Avg Tokens 0) all flood solid green — the encoding literally says "zero tokens wins, three times." And the item section defaults to "All Items," so you scroll past identical triple-pass cards to find the two disagreements the page exists to show.
  **Better:** open with a "what changed" delta summary against a baseline; default the items to *differences only*.
- **Models page fails at both entry states.** Filtered narrow, it renders one mid-flight run as a 14-tile card declaring `PASS@1 0%, CONSISTENCY NA, RELIABILITY NA` — a teammate glancing at this concludes gpt-4o is broken. Set the window to All and it surrenders: "Select a Task and Dataset — use the filters above" ([77-models-all.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/77-models-all.png)) — a page whose *required input* is a generic filter toolbar.
  **Better:** first-class task/dataset scope pickers in the header, "latest K completed runs per model" as the data window, and a ranked sortable table instead of tile-soup cards.
- **"Charts" contains no time axis.** It's horizontal bars per run — a snapshot ranking. The eval maintainer's job, *watch regressions over time*, has no home anywhere in the product. This is the single largest functional hole.
  **Better:** metric-over-time lines per model, x = run date/version, clickable run markers.
- **No compare affordance on run detail.** The most frequent transition in the product (run → baseline) requires going *back* to the list, entering checkbox-selection mode, and clicking a toolbar button.
  **Better:** "Compare vs previous/baseline" in the run hero.

### Flow

- **Onboarding is severed at every link.** Empty project → a CLI snippet with placeholder args; no `pip install`, no mention an API key is needed (it's the third tab of Settings), no copy button, no docs link ([50-empty-runs.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/50-empty-runs.png)). A first-timer cannot reach a first run without leaving the app.
  **Better:** a 3-step checklist (install → mint key inline → pre-filled command with real base URL).
- **Approval flow has no rationale capture and almost no record.** Reject reasons surface only as a tooltip; the rejected run's detail page shows a gray REJECTED pill with **no reason, reviewer, date, or link** anywhere on it ([33-run-detail-rejected-rootcause.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/33-run-detail-rejected-rootcause.png)). The engineer who must fix the run can't see why it was rejected from the run itself.
- **Priority inversion confirmed:** deleting a RUNNING run is two clicks; approving a submitted run requires discovering a hidden icon popover past a horizontal scroll.

## 4. Tier ② — Is it a coherent system?

**The design language, stated:** *terminal-dark instrument panel — near-black surfaces, monospace for the data plane, ALL-CAPS micro-labels, one teal primary, colored status pills, chips for everything.* It's a real, recognizable point of view — **started, not applied**. The shell is shared; the *semantics* are re-decided per page.

### Consistency matrix (verified across ~40 screens; key hexes pixel-checked)

| Component | Verdict | Specifics |
|---|---|---|
| Primary button | ✅ consistent | One teal-filled primary everywhere, no competing accent — real system bones |
| Card grids (projects/datasets) | ✅ consistent | Same anatomy: title + mono slug + badge + footer stats |
| Empty states | ✅ mostly | Shared ∅-glyph family with job-aware escalation (CLI on runs, CTA on datasets) — but commitment varies: CTA / snippet / text-only / bare across the six pages |
| **Status pills** | ❌ **divergent** | COMPLETED is **purple `#6343F7`** on project tables, **green `#10B981`** in Admin (green = APPROVED elsewhere); APPROVED/REJECTED collapse to the **same neutral gray `#A8A8BA`** on the run hero and compare cards — verdicts go gray exactly where the reviewer decides |
| **Destructive actions** (the canary) | ❌ **divergent** | Five costumes: red ghost text (selection toolbar), icon-only trash (rows), filled red Delete beside a green ✓ (review cards), filled red Disable/Archive (admin), Danger-Zone red. Only the bulk path confirms. Archive is calm gray in Danger Zone but alarm-red filled in Admin — same verb, opposite severity signal |
| Confirmation truthfulness | ❌ divergent | Delete modal: *"This action cannot be undone"* — while the sidebar's own **Deleted Runs** page says soft-deleted runs are restorable. One of them is lying |
| Tabs | ❌ divergent | Three paradigms: underline tabs (settings/admin/datasets), stat-cards-that-are-secretly-tabs (Reviews — visually identical to *static* stat cards elsewhere), segmented controls |
| Filters/search | ❌ divergent | Global filter bar (Runs/Charts/Models) vs bespoke Reviews row (chips + dual sliders) vs bare datasets search vs a fourth item-feed toolbar on run detail |
| Tables | ⚠️ minor drift | One visual grammar, but only datasets gets page-size + kebab menus; the far busier runs table gets neither |
| Item cards (run detail vs compare) | ⚠️ minor drift | Same component family, flipped label casing (ALL-CAPS vs sentence case) and numbering — classic fork-and-drift |
| Pagination | ⚠️ minor drift | Three dialects (wording, arrows, placement, capability) |
| Forms | ⚠️ minor drift | Micro-labeled in settings/modals; placeholder-only in admin/member inline rows |
| Buttons (secondary tier) | ⚠️ minor drift | Gray-outline secondary vs underlined-link-in-button-chrome ("Open"/"Settings") vs purple-outline (Compare/Cohort/Auto-Analyze) |
| Accent semantics | ❌ divergent | Purple simultaneously means COMPLETED, AI, and compare — so it means nothing |

**Verdict: look-alikes that drift.** And the seams date the hands: a dense mono core (runs/run-detail/compare), a visibly newer and cleaner datasets generation (breadcrumb heroes, kebabs, page-size — the newest patterns landed on the *least-trafficked* pages), an older utility layer (Trash, Admin), and Reviews as a fourth island that invents three patterns in one screen.

## 5. Tier ③ — Polish (each tied upward)

- **Color anarchy (→ ②):** eight accent hues on one runs-list viewport; task names in decorative magenta; latency histogram buckets tinted green→red purely by bucket *order*, implying judgment that isn't there. Pass/fail — the one semantic an eval tool must protect — drowns.
- **The EXPECTED OUTPUT slab (→ ① hierarchy error):** the static reference answer is a solid saturated-green block — the loudest element of every item card — while the model's output is muted and the verdict is a 4px dot ([70-run-approved-scroll-2.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/70-run-approved-scroll-2.png)). Green-fill = "success" everywhere else; here it decorates a constant.
- **Trace Stats wall of zeros (→ ① state-blindness):** 8–10 prime above-the-fold tiles reading `0 / 0.0 / —` on every untraced run, plus a "0 TOKENS" lozenge on every card, instead of one line saying "no trace data — add instrumentation."
- **Live runs painted with final-verdict colors (→ ①):** one scored item of twelve renders an alarm-orange 48.3% donut indistinguishable from a finished verdict ([36-run-live.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/36-run-live.png)).
- **UUIDs as identity (→ ①):** the breadcrumb of every run is a UUID; on the FAILED run the *page H1 itself* is the UUID, with the human name demoted to a "RUN NAME · METADATA" chip — at the user's moment of highest stress ([35-run-detail-failed.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/35-run-detail-failed.png)).
- **Shipped text corruption (one-off, but telling):** mojibake glyphs where "P50" should render, directly under the largest number on the most-visited screen (live + failed runs).
- **Copy was never proofread (→ ② no copy standard):** "SELECTED 1 RUNS"; Profile permanently announces a past migration ("Your access is **now** project-scoped…"); button casing tracks page age ("New Project" vs "New dataset").
- **Settings General loads a populated project as empty fields with a live Save button** ([22-project-settings.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/22-project-settings.png)) — verified real, reads as data loss on the exact page where trust matters.
- **Mobile is broken, not degraded (→ ② no breakpoints exist):** stat chips overstrike the title, the table collapses to one column, the fixed sidebar eats half the viewport ([60-mobile-runs-list.png](tmp/design-audit/screens/60-mobile-runs-list.png)) — and checking a live run from a phone is a real job here.
- **Two different 404s on adjacent routes; the docs portal is a different brand entirely.**

Genuine credit where due: the runs table's live pill (`RUNNING · 33% · 4/12`), the Display→Columns menu (search + grouped sections — survives 30 metrics), the score-distribution histograms that expose bimodality a mean hides, typed infrastructure errors with retry history, the root-cause→solution "Failure to Fix Flow," and the Reviews empty state that teaches the whole corrections model in two sentences. These are Linear-grade moments.

## 6. The highest-leverage changes, ranked

1. **Build the approval queue and put Approve/Reject on the run hero** — a "Run approvals" tab under Review, comment-on-reject, decision banner on the run page. The product's differentiating governance feature currently has no surface. *(① wrong design)*
2. **Restructure run detail: failures first, table not feed** — verdict band, then a dense failures-first item table with row-expand; collapse empty analysis sections; suppress zero-telemetry Trace Stats. This is the screen the daily user lives in. *(① wrong design)*
3. **Give regression-over-time a home** — replace "Charts" with real time-series per model/metric, and fold Charts/Models/Dashboard into one Analysis surface. *(① wrong design)*
4. **Create a StatusBadge + destructive-action system of record** — one status→hue map (admin included; APPROVED/REJECTED keep their colors on heroes), one confirm component with truthful copy ("moves to Deleted Runs — admins can restore"), one severity tiering for Archive/Disable/Delete. *(② inconsistency)*
5. **Fix run identity** — human names as H1/breadcrumb/selection-chips everywhere, UUID behind a copy affordance; stop auto-concatenating model+date into names that duplicate adjacent columns. *(① + ③)*
6. **Make Compare open with the verdict** — baseline selector, "what changed" delta summary, differences-only default; neutralize the green-flood matrix. *(① suboptimal)*
7. **Repair the first-run path** — 3-step in-app checklist (install → mint API key inline → pre-filled command), and hydrate the Settings form. *(① flow + ③ trust)*

---

## 7. Best and worst

**Single best design decision: Compare's item-by-item layout** — shared INPUT/EXPECTED rendered once, per-run output columns side-by-side, verdict + latency + metric chips per column, root-cause card inline on the failing output. It holds the constant constant and lets the eye scan what varies; it's the structurally correct answer to the product's hardest question, and most eval tools never find it. (Runner-up: project root landing directly on the Runs workbench.)

**Single worst structural decision: the run detail page's inverted pyramid + card feed.** The page that defines this product buries its object of work — failing items — under four screens of aggregates *including empty placeholder sections*, then renders the items as ~430px cards (two per viewport) in which the loudest visual element is the static expected answer rather than the failure. It misorders the page against the user's question, picks a pattern that collapses at realistic dataset sizes, and inverts the card's own hierarchy — a Tier-① miss on the highest-traffic screen, which no amount of consistency or polish would redeem.
